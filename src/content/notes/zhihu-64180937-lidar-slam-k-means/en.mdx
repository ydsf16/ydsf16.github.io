K-means partitions samples into a chosen number of clusters by alternating centroid assignment and centroid updates. It is a compact example of iterative optimization and can help organize point-cloud measurements.

Its outcome depends on initialization, the selected cluster count, and the distance metric. Multiple initializations and domain-specific features often make it more useful for real sensor data.
