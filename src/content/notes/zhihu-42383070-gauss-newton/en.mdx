Gauss–Newton minimizes a nonlinear least-squares objective by linearizing residuals around the current estimate. At each iteration, it solves the normal equations for an increment and updates the parameters.

In SLAM, it is the basic engine behind pose estimation and bundle adjustment. Its speed comes from ignoring second-order residual terms; it works well near a good solution, while damping, robust kernels, and careful initialization improve behavior on difficult data.
