Givens rotations solve a linear least-squares system $A x=b$ by successively zeroing selected entries of $A$. Each rotation affects only two rows, which makes the method easy to implement and useful for incremental or sparse updates.

Applying the same orthogonal rotations to $b$ transforms the system into an upper-triangular one. The solution is then obtained by back substitution. Orthogonal transformations preserve the least-squares objective, so the method is numerically stable and closely related to QR decomposition.
