> Notes while reading Joan Solà’s *Quaternion Kinematics for the Error-State Kalman Filter*. This is a condensed study record of the source material.

## 1. Quaternion definition and basic properties

### Definition

A quaternion has one real part and three imaginary parts:

$$
Q=q_w+q_xi+q_yj+q_zk\in\mathbb H.
$$

Equivalent notation is

$$
Q=q_w+\mathbf q_v
=\langle q_w,\mathbf q_v\rangle
=[q_w,q_x,q_y,q_z]^{\mathsf T}.
$$

A quaternion with zero real part is pure imaginary; one with zero vector part is a real number.

### Properties

**Sum**

$$
\mathbf p+\mathbf q=
\begin{bmatrix}p_w+q_w\\\mathbf p_v+\mathbf q_v\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**Product**

$$
\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q=
\begin{bmatrix}
p_wq_w-\mathbf p_v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_v\\
p_w\mathbf q_v+q_w\mathbf p_v+\mathbf p_v\times\mathbf q_v
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Quaternion multiplication is not commutative,

$$
\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q\ne\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf p,
$$

but is associative and distributive:

$$
(\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q)\otimes\mathbf r
=
\mathbf p\otimes(\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf r),
$$

$$
\mathbf p\otimes(\mathbf q+\mathbf r)
=
\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q+\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf r,
\qquad
(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)\otimes\mathbf r
=
\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf r+\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf r.
$$

Products can be written as matrix multiplication:

$$
\mathbf q_1\otimes\mathbf q_2
=
[\mathbf q_1]_L\mathbf q_2
=
[\mathbf q_2]_R\mathbf q_1,
$$

$$
[\mathbf q]_L=
q_w\mathbf I+
\begin{bmatrix}
0&-\mathbf q_v^{\mathsf T}\\
\mathbf q_v&[\mathbf q_v]_\times
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
[\mathbf q]_R=
q_w\mathbf I+
\begin{bmatrix}
0&-\mathbf q_v^{\mathsf T}\\
\mathbf q_v&-[\mathbf q_v]_\times
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The cross-product matrix is

$$
[\mathbf a]_\times=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&-a_z&a_y\\
a_z&0&-a_x\\
-a_y&a_x&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=[\mathbf a]_\times\mathbf b.
$$

**Identity, conjugate, norm, and inverse**

$$
\mathbf 1=
\begin{bmatrix}1\\\mathbf0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf q^*=
\begin{bmatrix}q_w\\-\mathbf q_v\end{bmatrix}.
$$

$$
\mathbf q^*\otimes\mathbf q
=
\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf q^*
=
q_w^2+q_x^2+q_y^2+q_z^2,
\qquad
(\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q)^*
=
\mathbf q^*\otimes\mathbf p^*.
$$

$$
\lVert\mathbf q\rVert
=
\sqrt{\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf q^*}
=
\sqrt{q_w^2+q_x^2+q_y^2+q_z^2},
\qquad
\lVert\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q\rVert
=
\lVert\mathbf p\rVert\lVert\mathbf q\rVert.
$$

$$
\mathbf q^{-1}
=
\frac{\mathbf q^*}{\lVert\mathbf q\rVert^2}.
$$

For a unit quaternion, inverse equals conjugate:

$$
\mathbf q^{-1}=\mathbf q^*,
\qquad
\mathbf q=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta\\
\mathbf u\sin\theta
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Unit quaternions represent rigid-body rotations.

### Useful identities

The quaternion commutator is

$$
\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf q-\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf p
=
2\mathbf p_v\times\mathbf q_v.
$$

For pure quaternions,

$$
\mathbf p_v\otimes\mathbf q_v
=
-\mathbf p_v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_v+\mathbf p_v\times\mathbf q_v,
\qquad
\mathbf q_v\otimes\mathbf q_v=-\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert^2.
$$

If $\mathbf v=\mathbf u\theta$, then

$$
\mathbf v^2=-\theta^2,\quad
\mathbf v^3=-\mathbf u\theta^3,\quad
\mathbf v^4=\theta^4,\quad
\mathbf v^5=\mathbf u\theta^5.
$$

Therefore, the exponential of a pure quaternion is

$$
e^{\mathbf v}
=
e^{\mathbf u\theta}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf v^k}{k!}
=
\cos\theta+\mathbf u\sin\theta.
$$

It is a unit quaternion. For a general quaternion,

$$
e^{\mathbf q}
=
e^{q_w+\mathbf q_v}
=
e^{q_w}
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert\\
\frac{\mathbf q_v}{\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert}
\sin\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

For a unit quaternion,

$$
\log\mathbf q
=
\mathbf u\theta,
\qquad
\mathbf u=\frac{\mathbf q_v}{\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert},
\qquad
\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert,q_w).
$$

For a general quaternion,

$$
\log\mathbf q
=
\log\lVert\mathbf q\rVert+\mathbf u\theta.
$$

Quaternion powers follow:

$$
\mathbf q^t=
\exp(t\log\mathbf q),
\qquad
\mathbf q^t=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(t\theta)\\
\mathbf u\sin(t\theta)
\end{bmatrix}
\quad\text{for a unit quaternion}.
$$

## 2. Rotations and cross-relations

The rotation group $\operatorname{SO}(3)$ preserves vector norms, angles, and orientation:

$$
\lVert r(\mathbf v)\rVert=\lVert\mathbf v\rVert,
\qquad
\langle r(\mathbf v),r(\mathbf w)\rangle
=
\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf w\rangle,
$$

$$
\mathbf u\times\mathbf v=\mathbf w
\Longleftrightarrow
r(\mathbf u)\times r(\mathbf v)=r(\mathbf w).
$$

### Matrix representation of $\operatorname{SO}(3)$

$$
r(\mathbf v)=\mathbf R\mathbf v,
\qquad
\mathbf R^{\mathsf T}\mathbf R=\mathbf I,
\qquad
\mathbf R^{-1}=\mathbf R^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\det\mathbf R=1.
$$

The exponential maps are

$$
\exp:\mathfrak{so}(3)\rightarrow\operatorname{SO}(3),
\qquad
[\mathbf v]_\times\mapsto e^{[\mathbf v]_\times},
$$

$$
\operatorname{Exp}:\mathbb R^3\rightarrow\operatorname{SO}(3),
\qquad
\mathbf v\mapsto\operatorname{Exp}(\mathbf v)=e^{[\mathbf v]_\times}.
$$

Rodrigues’ formula for $\mathbf v=\phi\mathbf u$ is

$$
\mathbf R
=
\mathbf I+\sin\phi[\mathbf u]_\times+
(1-\cos\phi)[\mathbf u]_\times^2
=
\mathbf I\cos\phi+
[\mathbf u]_\times\sin\phi+
\mathbf u\mathbf u^{\mathsf T}(1-\cos\phi).
$$

The logarithmic maps are

$$
\log:\operatorname{SO}(3)\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}(3),
\qquad
\log(\mathbf R)=[\mathbf u\phi]_\times,
$$

$$
\phi=\arccos\left(\frac{\operatorname{trace}(\mathbf R)-1}{2}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf u=\frac{(\mathbf R-\mathbf R^{\mathsf T})^\vee}{2\sin\phi},
$$

$$
\operatorname{Log}:\operatorname{SO}(3)\rightarrow\mathbb R^3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Log}(\mathbf R)=\mathbf u\phi
=
\log(\mathbf R)^\vee.
$$

### Quaternion representation of $\operatorname{SO}(3)$

The quaternion exponential is

$$
\exp:\mathbb H_p\rightarrow S^3,
\qquad
\mathbf V\mapsto e^{\mathbf V}.
$$

![Quaternion exponential map](/media/notes/zhihu-67872858-quaternion-kinematics-for-eskf-part-1/image-01.jpg)

The capitalized map is

$$
\operatorname{Exp}:\mathbb R^3\rightarrow S^3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Exp}(\mathbf v)=e^{\mathbf v/2}.
$$

Thus

$$
\dot{\mathbf q}=\frac12\mathbf q\otimes\boldsymbol\omega,
\qquad
\mathbf q=e^{\boldsymbol\omega t/2},
$$

and for a rotation vector $\phi\mathbf u$,

$$
\mathbf q
=
\operatorname{Exp}(\phi\mathbf u)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(\phi/2)\\
\mathbf u\sin(\phi/2)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The quaternion logarithms are

$$
\log(\mathbf q)=\mathbf u\theta,
\qquad
\operatorname{Log}(\mathbf q)=\mathbf u\phi=2\log(\mathbf q),
$$

$$
\phi=2\operatorname{atan2}(\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert,q_w),
\qquad
\mathbf u=\frac{\mathbf q_v}{\lVert\mathbf q_v\rVert}.
$$

![Quaternion–rotation-matrix relation](/media/notes/zhihu-67872858-quaternion-kinematics-for-eskf-part-1/image-02.jpg)

Spherical linear interpolation is

$$
\mathbf q(t)
=
\mathbf q_0\otimes
(\mathbf q_0^*\otimes\mathbf q_1)^t
=
\mathbf q_0\otimes
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(t\Delta\phi/2)\\
\mathbf u\sin(t\Delta\phi/2)
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\mathbf R(t)
=
\mathbf R_0\operatorname{Exp}
\left(t\operatorname{Log}(\mathbf R_0^{\mathsf T}\mathbf R_1)\right).
$$

## 4. Perturbations, derivatives, and integrals

### Plus and minus operators on $\operatorname{SO}(3)$

Define plus by

$$
\mathbf S=\mathbf R\oplus\boldsymbol\theta
=
\mathbf R\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta),
$$

with equivalent quaternion form

$$
\mathbf q_s=\mathbf q_r\oplus\boldsymbol\theta
=
\mathbf q_r\otimes\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta).
$$

Define minus by

$$
\boldsymbol\theta
=
\mathbf S\ominus\mathbf R
=
\operatorname{Log}(\mathbf R^{-1}\mathbf S),
$$

$$
\boldsymbol\theta
=
\mathbf q_s\ominus\mathbf q_r
=
\operatorname{Log}(\mathbf q_r^*\otimes\mathbf q_s).
$$

### Four derivative definitions

For vector-space functions,

$$
\frac{\partial f(\mathbf x)}{\partial\mathbf x}
=
\lim_{\delta\mathbf x\to0}
\frac{f(\mathbf x+\delta\mathbf x)-f(\mathbf x)}
{\delta\mathbf x},
$$

$$
f(\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x)
\approx
f(\mathbf x)+
\frac{\partial f(\mathbf x)}{\partial\mathbf x}\Delta\mathbf x.
$$

For $\operatorname{SO}(3)\rightarrow\operatorname{SO}(3)$,

$$
\frac{\partial f(\mathbf R)}{\partial\boldsymbol\theta}
=
\lim_{\delta\boldsymbol\theta\to0}
\frac{
f(\mathbf R\oplus\delta\boldsymbol\theta)\ominus f(\mathbf R)
}{\delta\boldsymbol\theta},
$$

$$
f(\mathbf R\oplus\Delta\boldsymbol\theta)
\approx
f(\mathbf R)
\operatorname{Exp}
\left(
\frac{\partial f(\mathbf R)}{\partial\boldsymbol\theta}
\Delta\boldsymbol\theta
\right).
$$

For vector-space to $\operatorname{SO}(3)$,

$$
\frac{\partial f(\mathbf x)}{\partial\mathbf x}
=
\lim_{\delta\mathbf x\to0}
\frac{f(\mathbf x+\delta\mathbf x)\ominus f(\mathbf x)}
{\delta\mathbf x}.
$$

For $\operatorname{SO}(3)$ to vector space,

$$
\frac{\partial f(\mathbf R)}{\partial\boldsymbol\theta}
=
\lim_{\delta\boldsymbol\theta\to0}
\frac{
f(\mathbf R\operatorname{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol\theta))-f(\mathbf R)
}{\delta\boldsymbol\theta}.
$$

### Useful rotation Jacobians

$$
\frac{\partial(\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf a\otimes\mathbf q^*)}
{\partial\mathbf a}
=
\frac{\partial(\mathbf R\mathbf a)}{\partial\mathbf a}
=
\mathbf R.
$$

For $\mathbf q=[w,\mathbf v]^{\mathsf T}$,

$$
\frac{\partial(\mathbf q\otimes\mathbf a\otimes\mathbf q^*)}
{\partial\mathbf q}
=
2\begin{bmatrix}
w\mathbf a+\mathbf v\times\mathbf a&
\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf a\mathbf I+
\mathbf v\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}-
\mathbf a\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}-
w[\mathbf a]_\times
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The right Jacobian of $\operatorname{SO}(3)$ is

$$
\mathbf J_r(\boldsymbol\theta)
=
\lim_{\delta\boldsymbol\theta\to0}
\frac{
\operatorname{Log}
\left(
\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta)^{\mathsf T}
\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta+\delta\boldsymbol\theta)
\right)
}{\delta\boldsymbol\theta}.
$$

It enables first-order expansions:

$$
\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta+\delta\boldsymbol\theta)
\approx
\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta)
\operatorname{Exp}
\left(\mathbf J_r(\boldsymbol\theta)\delta\boldsymbol\theta\right),
$$

$$
\operatorname{Log}
\left(
\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\theta)
\operatorname{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol\theta)
\right)
\approx
\boldsymbol\theta+
\mathbf J_r^{-1}(\boldsymbol\theta)\delta\boldsymbol\theta.
$$

### Time derivatives and integration

$$
\dot{\mathbf q}
=
\frac12\boldsymbol\Omega(\boldsymbol\omega_L)\mathbf q
=
\frac12\mathbf q\otimes\boldsymbol\omega_L,
\qquad
\dot{\mathbf R}=\mathbf R[\boldsymbol\omega_L]_\times.
$$

Zeroth-order integration is

$$
\mathbf q_{n+1}
\approx
\mathbf q_n\otimes
\operatorname{Exp}(\boldsymbol\omega_n\Delta t)
$$

for forward Euler, or use $\boldsymbol\omega_{n+1}$ for backward Euler. Midpoint integration uses

$$
\bar{\boldsymbol\omega}
=
\frac{\boldsymbol\omega_{n+1}+\boldsymbol\omega_n}{2},
\qquad
\mathbf q_{n+1}
\approx
\mathbf q_n\otimes
\operatorname{Exp}(\bar{\boldsymbol\omega}\Delta t).
$$

The first-order approximation is

$$
\mathbf q_{n+1}
\approx
\mathbf q_n\otimes
\left(
\operatorname{Exp}(\bar{\boldsymbol\omega}\Delta t)
+
\frac{\Delta t^2}{24}
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
\boldsymbol\omega_n\times\boldsymbol\omega_{n+1}
\end{bmatrix}
\right).
$$
